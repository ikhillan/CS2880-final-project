\begin{frame}{The new rental replay}
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item Reconstruct all 1,280 available markets across three windows and two designs.
Each market has 64 renters and 64 listings; 320 planned markets fail the original Staten Island supply check.
\item Verify 55,115 cached GPT candidate hashes and all 54 original placement,
anchored-placement and menu-change means. No new model calls.
\item Nine fixed policies; 20 tie permutations for each of two anchored policies.
All policies preserve menu lengths and select only feasible options.
\item Pair on markets. Bootstrap 20 seed blocks, with 10,000 shared draws.
Report simultaneous intervals over four comparisons $\times$ two endpoints per window/design.
\end{itemize}
\source{Post-hoc sensitivity analysis on reused evaluation panels, not a newly untouched confirmatory test. Preferences, screening and allocations are simulated. Source: findings/rental-anchor-access/ and experiments/rental\_anchor\_access\_export.py.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A coordinated baseline with exact optimization}
Let $x_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$ say whether renter $i$ sees listing $j$; $\ell_j=\sum_i x_{ij}$.
First solve
\[
\min_x\ \sum_j \ell_j^2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\sum_j x_{ij}=\min\{5,|E_i|\},\qquad x_{ij}=0\text{ if }j\notin E_i.
\]
\small
Among load-minimizers, maximize recommendation rank score.
Increasing marginal costs $1,3,5,\ldots$ encode $\ell_j^2$ as an exact min-cost flow.

\medskip
Cached GPT order supplies scores; unshown feasible options tie.
The policy sees neither realized preferences nor landlord priorities nor reference assignments.

\medskip
Same menu lengths as the anchor; potentially more edits. Neither objective guarantees DA placement or individual retention.
\source{experiments\_jobs/coordinated\_policies.py. Rental listings have unit capacity. Model-informed score variants additionally know the simulator's deterministic utility coefficients.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What the published jobs totals can establish}
\small
With fixed strict preferences and priorities, editing $C$ applicants' menus
can cause at most $C$ gross exits and $C$ gross entrants under applicant-proposing DA.
If $\Delta=P_1-P_0$, then
\[
0\le X\le\min\{P_0,\ N-P_1,\ C-\max(\Delta,0)\}.
\]

\begin{frame}{Broader considerations and sources}
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Access.} Individual retention can preserve existing exclusion.
Report outcomes for applicants without an initial offer alongside incumbent safety.
\item \textbf{Behavior.} Families may ignore advice, add options, or change ranks.
Fixed-report replays do not identify deployment effects.
\item \textbf{Data and participation.} Keep pupil-level records private and respect
source restrictions. Any field intervention needs appropriate institutional review
and a plan for participant information, correction and complaints.
\item \textbf{Resources.} Recommendations redistribute access to existing capacity.
They do not create affordable apartments, job vacancies, or school seats.
\end{itemize}

\href{https://neurips.cc/public/EthicsGuidelines}{NeurIPS ethics guidelines};
\href{https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook}{NeurIPS 2026 handbook: checklist}.
These motivate consideration of harms; our proposed safeguards have not been tested.}
\end{frame}
