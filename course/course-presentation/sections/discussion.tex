\section{Housing, prior work, and the next step}
\begin{frame}[t,label=housing]{Housing and AI recommendations}
\small
A housing recommender could determine which feasible options reach a client.
In our model, those options form $E_0$.

\medskip
The audit problem would apply to a service that can open additional options
and uses DA to assign clients. We have not established that a housing platform
operates this way.

\medskip
Our experiments measure the effects of synthetic access restrictions.
They do not evaluate an AI recommender or a real housing intervention.
\sources{Liu et al.\ (2024) audit LLM housing recommendations for racial steering.
A proposed listing study and access-cost objective appear in Appendices 14--15.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t,label=ending]{Conclusions}
\small
Making a stable matching available can cost much less than making
DA select one.

\medskip
Choosing the audits is NP-hard. Our exact method learns constraints
from failed DA runs and reports a verified plan and a cost bound.

\medskip
On the synthetic markets, simple heuristics are competitive.
Even a cheapest successful plan can leave some clients worse off.

\medskip
The housing application needs a service whose access rules and matching
process fit the model.
\sources{Stability is a guarantee about the input rankings. It does not guarantee
fair rankings, truthful preferences, or improvements for every client.}
\end{frame}
