# Development matching comparison

This is an exploratory diagnostic designed after the team observed scarcity. It uses 40 independent seed blocks in the 12 supported June–August 2026 cells across Bronx, Brooklyn, Manhattan, and Queens. It measures matching consequences under two explicit shortlist policies and two modeled listing priority rules. The held-out donors, held-out catalogue months and AI recommender play no role.

## Reproduction

Run the companion scarcity input preparation, which produces the three FirstMover CSVs and the Census aggregate donor histogram. From the manuscript's artifact directory, invoke:

```sh
python nyc-matching-dev/run.py --data /path/to/nyc-scarcity/data --catalogue-module /path/to/nyc-scarcity/scarcity.py --out nyc-matching-dev/results.json
```

Only NumPy and the Python standard library are needed. The catalogue loader imports its sibling `degree.py`. The run records input hashes and NumPy/Python versions; ZIPs and local file times are not used as seeds. The full reproducibility package includes the scarcity loader and donor preparation code. Raw monthly listing files represent records at first appearance. [FirstMover](https://www.firstmovernyc.com/open-data) · [Census PUMS](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/microdata/documentation.html) · [BLS CPI-U](https://www.bls.gov/regions/mid-atlantic/data/consumerpriceindexhistorical_us_table.htm)

## Order of operations

1. Load the 70% calibration donor split and ordinary listing pools cleaned by the companion scarcity analysis. For each borough-month and block, sample 64 renters with replacement using household weights and 64 listings uniformly without replacement. Use asking rents in July 2026 dollars, all positive prices, the 40% rent limit and minimum bedrooms `max(0, (household_size-1)//2)`. A household is lower-income when its 2024 adjusted income is at most $64,435.014, the cash-renter median. Staten Island had fewer than 64 listings per development cell.
2. Independently draw a standard normal quality for each listing and a standard normal taste for each renter–listing pair. Renter utility is minus twice the rent-to-monthly-budget ratio, plus half the number of spare bedrooms capped at two, plus half the common quality draw, plus 0.75 times pair taste. This is a simple prespecified-for-this-diagnostic scoring rule. Its scores are simulated. Common listing priority is one uniform strict lottery shared by all listings. Heterogeneous listing priority uses independent uniform strict lotteries for each listing. In each market both priorities use the same renters, listings, renter rankings and shortlists.
3. Uniform picks up to five distinct feasible listings at random per renter; cheapest picks the five feasible listings with lowest asking rent or all those available. Break price ties by a market-specific random listing order. Recommendations only select the visible edges. Renter-proposing DA on these edges follows the rent, space and taste ranking.
4. Compute blocking pairs on all feasible edges, provisional matched share, unconditional top-three rate, and mean normalized rank score (zero when unmatched; otherwise one minus the zero-based full-list rank divided by feasible degree). Verify that no visible pair blocks. Run DA from both sides on the full graph, verify full stability, verify that the full matched sets agree and check the ordering of extremal ranks for every renter. For common priorities, the extremal assignments must coincide.
5. A protected renter with a nonvacuous threshold is someone initially matched whose best full-stable assignment weakly improves upon their provisional one. The group receives a sufficient zero-premium certificate when each such renter's worst stable assignment weakly improves upon the provisional one. Consequently every full-stable repair meets those thresholds. `screen_pass_vacuous` counts markets without any such thresholds; `screen_pass_positive_threshold` counts passes with at least one. `screen_variable_interval` counts markets with a nonvacuous renter whose best and worst full-stable partners differ; `screen_pass_variable_interval` counts certified markets among those. Other markets require additional optimization to judge the premium.
6. Use NumPy SeedSequence([20261112, block, month index, borough index]) for zero-based blocks 0–39; months June, July, August; boroughs Bronx, Brooklyn, Manhattan, Queens. The first eight child generators select renters, listings, shared utility, private tastes, uniform shortlists, price ties, common priorities, and heterogeneous priorities. Raw group counts are across 480 markets. Standardized rates sum within-cell renter rates with equal month weights and ACS recent-mover borough shares renormalized to the four supported boroughs. Conditional incidence among `d>5` uses the ratio of the two standardized rates. Percentile intervals use 5,000 resamples of entire paired seed blocks with seed 19112026. They quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on these catalogues, donors and preference specifications.

The comparison's two empirical outcomes are renter-weighted rates in a simulated market with repeated household and listing records across independent draws. They are not observed choices or lease outcomes. Cheapest-first can produce substantial competition for the same listings, especially when higher-income renters can reach many more of them.
