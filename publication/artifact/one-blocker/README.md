# Exact degree-two repair and the number of initial blockers

Internal research note, November 13, 2026. AI-assisted argument and verification. It uses the one-to-one strict-preference model of the team record, with positive audit costs and symmetric acceptability. Each successful plan makes the proposer-DA response stable on the full graph. The protection threshold is the least possible set of proposer losses relative to the provisional response.

**One-blocker boundary.** Suppose both sides of the full graph have degree at most two. If the provisional DA matching has at most one full-market blocking pair, every successful audit plan already attains the unavoidable-loss set for every proposer group. In particular the protection premium is zero. This holds for arbitrary portfolios and initial vacancies.

**Proof.** Call a full component flexible if it admits two distinct stable matchings. Matched-set invariance implies that the symmetric difference of two stable matchings consists of alternating cycles. At degree two each such cycle occupies an entire component. A flexible component is thus an even cycle whose only two full stable outcomes are the perfect alternations D and S. In the proposer-optimal alternation D every proposer gets their first choice. Their partner changes between the two alternations, and they have just two neighbors. Every receiver must prefer their S edge, since otherwise their D edge would block S.

For any partial matching on such a cycle, a D edge blocks exactly when its receiver is unmatched. If that receiver has an S partner, the receiver prefers S. If that receiver is unmatched, its D proposer prefers it to any current assignment. By the same argument an S edge blocks exactly when its proposer is unmatched. As D and S are disjoint perfect matchings, a cycle with k unmatched participants on each side has exactly 2k blocking pairs. This identity holds for any matching of the cycle. In particular every flexible cycle of a provisional matching with at most one blocker across the full market must already be perfect. Its provisional matching is D or S. With D, the visible graph contains everyone's distinct first choices, and expansion retains them. With S, both successful outcomes weakly improve on the provisional ranks. On components with a unique full stable matching every successful outcome coincides with full DA. Consequently every group realizes its unavoidable losses on every successful plan. The two-blocker degree-two cycle in the companion degree-boundary folder has ordinary cost 1 and cost n−1 for protecting one proposer. Thus two initial blockers suffice in degree two and one is impossible for positive premium. The degree-three perfect-initial ring attains an unbounded premium with one blocker.

**The support model also becomes exact.** Fix a visible graph H and a full-stable matching M contained in H. M is stable on H. On each forest component of H its restriction is the unique H-stable outcome, since any two such outcomes differ on alternating cycles. Therefore DA(H) equals M on that component. Any cycle component retained by H is already an entire component of the full graph E, since E has maximum degree two. On an intact cycle DA is full-market stable. Hidden edges can only occur among components where DA(H) agrees with M, so they cannot block. Proposer thresholds on a group transfer from M to DA(H) by proposer optimality on H. Conversely, a successful protected response is itself a witness. A plan is consequently successful if and only if it supports a full-stable matching, also with protected thresholds. The equivalence works for arbitrary portfolios.

**A linear-time algorithm for singleton firms.** Compute proposer- and receiver-proposing DA on E and proposer DA on E₀. In each full component, the first two runs enumerate all possible distinct full-stable outcomes (one on a rigid component, two on a flexible cycle). Remove options violating at least one protected threshold. For each surviving matching M in a component calculate w(M), the sum over its initially hidden matching edges of the smaller of the two endpoint audit costs. These edges are vertex-disjoint. With one client per firm, choosing a cheapest endpoint of each edge attains this cost. Every plan that supports M must pay at least this sum. Independent choices across full components give the global optimum; return the union of the explicit endpoint choices. The graph scans and three DA runs take linear time in the explicit input, measured in arithmetic operations on the cost encoding. Ties in cost can be settled deterministically.

In particular, the premium is a sum over flexible cycles. A cycle contributes max(0, w(D) − w(S)) exactly when G contains a proposer provisionally holding their D partner. Every other cycle contributes zero. The rigid-component costs cancel. If the provisional matching on a contributing cycle is D then w(D)=0, so only imperfect cycles can have a positive contribution.

**Checks.** The companion verifier tests all strict profiles on all labeled 3-by-3 degree-at-most-two full graphs. It independently enumerates full stable partial matchings, compares support with the actual response on every visible graph, and tests the one-blocker boundary on every expansion of every such baseline. For a fixed positive nonuniform vector of singleton-firm costs, it compares the componentwise formula against enumerated audit optima for each of the eight possible protected groups and every baseline. Counts and the first concrete imperfect two-blocker example are in results.json. The verifier imports only the DA and bitmask helper routines from the companion degree-boundary folder.
