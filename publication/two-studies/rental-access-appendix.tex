\section{Public rental coordination and access replay}\label{app:rental-access}
\paragraph{Panels and timing.}
The development window uses June--August 2026 ads and calibration donors;
the original held-out window uses September--October 2026 ads and evaluation
donors; replication uses June--August 2025 ads and evaluation donors.
Each supply design has 240, 160 and 240 supported markets, respectively,
with 64 renters and 64 unit-capacity listings. The original 320 Staten Island
supply failures remain omitted and recorded; no replacement markets are
drawn. The analysis was specified on November 22, 2026 after the original
placement results were known. It is a post-hoc simulation audit of all three
windows, including the ordinary design with limited feasible supply.

\paragraph{Information and algorithms.}
Random, GPT and the transparent observable-score rule each show at most five
feasible listings. Anyone with at most five sees their full feasible set.
The original anchor $A$ maximizes matching cardinality and then reuse of
shown pairs; inserting its assigned listing replaces one existing option.
We reproduce the original GPT last-pick and random deletion conventions.
The same operation is applied to random and score menus.

For balancing, let $d_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$ indicate a displayed feasible pair,
$q_i$ the original menu length, and $\ell_j=\sum_i d_{ij}$.
The rule $B$ minimizes $\sum_j\ell_j^2$ subject to
$\sum_j d_{ij}=q_i$, then maximizes $\sum_{ij}s_{ij}d_{ij}$ among minimizers.
For $B(G)$, cached picks receive descending integer scores; unlisted options
tie at zero. This censored score convention does not assert that the unlisted
options have zero relevance. A min-cost flow represents each listing's
successive exposure costs by $1,3,5,\ldots$, with a bounded integer multiplier
making load minimization lexicographically prior to relevance. Unlike $A$,
$B$ can replace several menu positions. It guarantees neither maximum
matching cardinality nor DA recipient retention.

The model-informed controls $B(\mathrm{Score})$ and $A_s(G)$ use the
simulator's deterministic observable utility coefficients. The latter first
maximizes assignment cardinality, then normalized within-person score rank,
and inserts that anchor into GPT menus. These are stronger information
benchmarks, not independently learned relevance models. No policy observes
realized preference shocks, landlord priorities, full-list assignments or
baseline recipients. Table~\ref{tab:rental-controls} includes every fixed
policy, so the favorable score controls are not suppressed.

\paragraph{Inference.}
Each window/design has 20 seed blocks. We average market-specific conditional
coverage within seed and then equally across seeds; placement includes all
64 renters. The release separately labels pooled-recipient and unconditional
coverage. Shared 10,000-draw paired bootstrap resamples operate on whole seed
blocks. The primary family has four comparisons---$A(G)-G$,
$A(G)-\mathrm{Random}$, $A(G)-A(\mathrm{Random})$, and $B(G)-A(G)$---times
placement and conditional coverage. Within each window/design, simultaneous
intervals use the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute centered bootstrap
deviation across these eight estimates. They are approximate sensitivity
intervals, not finite-sample guarantees or a single adjustment across all
windows. Other controls and subgroup intervals are exploratory and pointwise.
Intervals describe variability across the specified simulations, not
uncertainty about NYC housing-market effects. Undefined conditional
denominators remain undefined in the distributed tables.

In enriched markets, balancing observable scores yields 81.56\%, 80.29\%
and 81.67\% placement, slightly above balancing GPT ranks in each window.
Its coverage change relative to $A(G)$ is $-1.40$, $+0.02$ and $-1.57$
points; the middle pointwise interval spans zero. The simulator-informed
ranker is therefore an important control, not evidence that GPT scores
are essential to the congestion improvement.

\paragraph{Individual outcomes.}
Exits count previously placed people who become unmatched; entrants count
the reverse transition. Their difference must equal the net placement gain.
The worse-rank endpoint treats every feasible listing as better than being
unmatched. Ranks and their normalized costs are ordinal summaries under the
fixed preference model, not measured welfare. Table~\ref{tab:rental-groups}
sums counts across the three windows without treating appearances as unique
households. Income rows partition the population, as do the four degree rows;
the two partitions must not be added together. Per-window subgroup estimates
and their support are retained in the source tables.

Balancing also creates benefits for unchanged-menu applicants. When
$q_i$ equals feasible degree, the full feasible set is the only permissible
menu. For the enriched held-out group's 2,328 appearances with one to five
options, $B(G)-A(G)$ gives 349 entrants and 15 exits. The mean seed-specific
placement gain is 14.41 points (exploratory pointwise interval
$[13.27,15.51]$). The corresponding gains are 14.73 and 14.91 points in
development and replication. This mechanism can help or hurt people whose
own choices are unchanged; it does not establish household welfare effects.

\paragraph{Anchor tie sensitivity.}
We repeat the GPT and random anchors under 20 fixed applicant/listing index
permutations per market. Each person's deletion order remains fixed within
this sensitivity. Counts are averaged within market before rates are formed;
the draws are not new independent markets. Table~\ref{tab:rental-ties} shows
that positive mean incumbent exits persist. Original-anchor levels are in
Table~\ref{tab:rental-controls}; the source tables also retain both randomized
anchors' paired contrasts and transition counts.

\paragraph{Verification and release.}
No new language-model calls were made. The reconstructed markets use public
listing/survey inputs and the committed GPT caches. The exporter verifies
55,115 cached candidate hashes and 54 original placement, anchored-placement
and menu-change means. All 36 downstream placement checks against the original
released tables also agree to numerical precision. The reconstruction checks
feasibility, personal menu lengths,
assignment headcounts and transition identities. The run manifest records
1,280 markets, 20 completed shards, input-packet and analysis-source hashes.
The paper capsule contains only aggregate outputs; its manifest authenticates
the six aggregate source files and compact input-provenance record. Run
\texttt{python3 rental-access/rebuild.py} to verify
them and regenerate every table in this section. The repository's
\texttt{findings/rental-anchor-access/} release supplies the protocol and
316,680 seed-level count rows, without household or listing records. Rebuilding
from those counts reproduces all released estimates and intervals within
$10^{-10}$; the release retains the comparison report. This replay
does not recover the missing jobs outcomes or measure responses to advice.
