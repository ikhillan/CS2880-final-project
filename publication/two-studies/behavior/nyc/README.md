# What happens when a household can pursue apartments outside the five shown?

We compare Haiku menus (B) and uniform five-listing menus (A) on the original 480 housing development markets. Renter preferences, listing priorities, affordability, inventory, and both original menus stay fixed within each market. Each design has 15,360 renter instances. A simulated household may recur across market draws. We vary how a household pursues the options on and off its shortlist. The data do not identify the frequency or order of off-list rental applications. These are exploratory scenarios with the original endpoints known. See `PLAN.md` and `TAIL-PLAN.md`.

**Independent adherence.** With probability p a renter pursues their assigned shortlist and with probability 1−p they pursue their entire feasible list. Either case follows the household’s original modeled preference order. The adherence draw for a given renter is exactly the same when all households receive A and when all households receive B. This differs from random adoption of A versus B in a single market. Each market uses 32 independent antithetic pairs at p=.25, .5, .75, with a common uniform draw across probabilities. The full-list outcome (p=0) and frozen pilot (p=1) are deterministic. This required 184,320 interior DA clears. We also test universal retrieval of the highest-ranked omitted listing, placing it in the original modeled preference order even if doing so puts it first.

Entries are B minus A in percentage points, using all renters in the denominator. Coverage asks whether the original full-list DA partner is present. First choice means the best apartment under the original modeled order on all feasible options. Every pair at half adherence has exactly the arithmetic midpoint of the p=0 and p=1 coverage levels in each arm; at other interior probabilities the table reports Monte Carlo estimates. We average applicants within each original borough/month, then apply the frozen ACS borough and equal month weights across the 12 supported cells. 95% block intervals resample the 20 paired simulation blocks 10,000 times. Conditional Monte Carlo standard errors treat the 32 antithetic pairs as independent. The intervals describe variation in the original model and data. At 50% adherence the ordinary and enriched placement effects have pointwise block intervals of [−1.22,−0.83] and [−1.90,−1.36] pp, respectively (conditional MCSE 0.013 and 0.019). Gross losses with identical effective lists remain 0.629 and 0.798 per 100 renters.

**Proposal order.** For each recommender, give each renter their originally displayed apartments first, in their original relative order. Append either one or all hidden feasible options, with multiple recovered options following original preference order. This behavioral assumption can separate an applicant’s proposal order from the order they use to evaluate an offer. Coverage tests the effective option set. Counts of gross losses with an identical effective menu require the complete ordered list to match. For both tail policies the renter still proposes to all options in turn if rejected. Clearinghouse DA is a model of competition in these simulated markets. Real leases and search behavior are unobserved.

Putting the recovered best option at its modeled rank gives pointwise first-choice intervals [−0.47,+0.22] ordinary and [−0.16,+0.54] enriched. The same option appended last yields [1.85,2.49] and [2.48,3.45]. Appending every option gives all renters the same feasible set in both arms. The ordinary placement difference is −0.38 pp [−0.66,−0.11] and the enriched difference is +0.28 pp [−0.07,+0.65]. In enriched markets the pooled unweighted gains minus losses are 379−462=−83, so the pooled sign differs from the geographically standardized point estimate. Among all renters, the share with at least one blocking pair under the original preferences at the full-tail assignment is 41.70% under uniform and 33.82% under Haiku in ordinary markets and 45.77% versus 33.43% in enriched markets. The algorithm clears on the order household applications actually follow in the scenario.

**Checks.** On each market and both tail lengths, we compared serial continuation from each prior assignment against a fresh simultaneous-proposal run on its modified orders. All 122,880 assignment comparisons over 1,920 allocations agreed. Every originally occupied listing is still occupied in its own arm. Each entrant was assigned one of the appended edges. Previously placed renters may lose their assignments. For example, after appending one option to the Haiku menus, 94 ordinary and 238 enriched previously placed renters went unplaced. For the other scenarios we compared 27,648 assignments and checked blocking pairs on the independent solver sample. All original full-adherence placement, coverage, and first-choice changes and their block interval endpoints reproduce the frozen paper exactly up to floating-point precision.

**Reproduction.** Install the upstream project's rental dependencies, restore the three June–August 2026 catalogs and Census donor profiles, and use its archived model cache. Set `REPO` to the project root with the first referee audit (commit 9660fdc or later). From this directory run

```sh
python rental_advice.py --repo "$REPO" --out output --workers 4
python rental_tail.py --repo "$REPO" --out output
python verify_advice.py output
```

Use `--workers 1` for serial execution. Parallel workers use POSIX fork. `nyc-advice-counts.npz` holds 11 counts per market, antithetic pair, orientation, and scenario. `nyc-markets.csv` gives the order and weights of markets. `nyc-tail-counts.csv` gives each deterministic arm and paired transition by market. No archive contains renter or apartment records or assignments. `verify_advice.py` recomputes all 168 response estimates and 96 tail estimates from these aggregates. `nyc-advice-results.csv` and `nyc-tail-results.csv` contain absolute levels, contrasts, and raw counts. A and B denote uniform and Haiku, respectively. In tail results `same_menu` requires identical order and `same_set` requires equal option sets. `enters_*` and `exits_*` compare an arm's own appended outcomes against its own original restricted outcomes. `gain` and `loss` compare B with A under the given response. All these indicators use all renter instances as their denominator for the `weighted_per100` column.

**Witnesses for changes under identical feasible sets.** In the all-options-tail scenario, 5,956 ordinary and 7,154 enriched renter instances were matched under both policies, had different assignments, preferred their A partner under A's proposal order, and preferred their B partner under B's proposal order. This pool exceeds gross losses (248/462) and gains (142/379). For each gross loss, follow its former apartment to its new occupant under B; when that occupant still prefers their prior apartment under B’s order, repeat. Within each direction every source had a distinct terminal satisfying both reversals. Reversing A and B certifies the gains. Across 480 markets all 1,231 paths were verified, with at most four steps. The two directions may share terminals. `nyc-full-tail-witnesses.csv` preserves counts and path lengths per market; `verify_advice.py` also reconciles these counts against the independent full-tail aggregates.
