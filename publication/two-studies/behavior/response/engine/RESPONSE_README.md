# What happens if families pursue more than their shown choices?

The four-cohort binding result is 4,638 more historically attended April schools in the rank-informed choice set and 1,670 fewer simulated placements among the same 1,969,120 linked applicants. Response changes the set pursued and can change the order of proposals. We count an April-school hit once if at least one pursued course is at that school. We also count placements, first choices among the original family-entered reports, initial-offer coverage, entries/exits and outcomes in the linked group without initial offers.

The underlying sources and admissions mechanism are credited in the paper's institutional release. The original predictors select among the submitted reports down to a pupil's secured course. Actual first-round reports may have choices below that course. Here the choice of pool reproduces the policy under study. In each simulated market the mechanism recalculates family promotion and priority from its own higher-grade assignments. Repeating the same attendance label across policy arms measures historical coverage; simulated choices and placements do not determine that label.

## Scenarios

- **endpoints** gives binding, all original choices pursued in original order or behind the shown choices, and the highest omitted course in original order or at the tail. Tail insertion can fall below a guaranteed course. A promoted family choice may nevertheless move earlier.
- **field** recovers a course at a previously unshown school. The top-ranked omitted new school and the omitted new school with the lowest congestion are separate scenarios. Congestion is the course's (eligible reported applications + 1)/(published total vacancies + 1); ties follow original rank. The family tries the new school in its original position or appends it after its display. Each pupil's Bernoulli response key is common across ranks, rates, and policies, independent across applicants and across eight repetitions. The 19.9% and 41.5% rates illustrate add-any levels from the external local warning study.
- **multiple** initially gives each responder one highest-ranked course at a previously unshown school. Independently, 57.3% seek a second such school if available, preserving submitted order among additions. Each school counts once, even if several of its courses are eligible. The two-point distribution matches a mean of 1.573 when every responder has at least two options.
- **risk** restricts those decisions to the same pupils under both policies. In each 2022–24 cohort, 64 separate family-by-course lotteries (batch 719) estimate nonplacement risk on the two binding menus. The broad screen includes pupils without a seat in at least 20 draws under both policies. The threshold neighborhood includes pupils without a seat in 13 through 25 draws under each policy. The final allocations for these screens use the recorded priorities. 2025 is the deterministic tie-breaking year.
- **reapplication** adds only explicitly re-listed C2 courses in the family's eligible C1 pool. In the tail scenario newly pursued courses are sorted by the actual complementary rank; alternatively we restore them at regular submission rank. The no-offer variant adds such courses only for historical pupils unoffered both initially and after the wait-list. Neither simulation anticipates unobserved new-to-report courses.
- **reference** independently constructs the ordered expanded contracts at each grade and uses simultaneous waves of DA proposals. It uses the published within-seat-type priority calculation but neither the serial DA queue nor the edge-reordering implementation. Eight scenarios for each policy and year agreed on every original course and seat category (32,714,384 applicant–policy comparisons). Equal unrestricted original-order reports also give identical arrays for both policies.
- **response-summary** reports each yearly result and the sum across the years in that design. Monte Carlo standard errors refer to the mean of paired simulated counts across eight respondent draws; deterministic runs have no Monte Carlo error. See the raw yearly rows for both policies' outcomes. Adding the two policy-specific counts of additional schools does not produce a count of distinct pupils across policies.

## Reproduction

Run the institutional historical input preparation from the full repository to obtain private numeric prefixes, two masks, seat and family sidecars, and register files. The institutional preparation for course-family lotteries produces the private course-family membership files. Run the complementary-round preparation to create `YYYY-relisted.i4` and `YYYY-unoffered.u1` aligned to that same numeric market. A C++17 compiler and Python 3.9+ are sufficient for this re-clearing step. Pass the previous institutional private directory as SIDE, REGISTERS and FAMILY when those files are together.

```sh
python3 reproduce_response.py --publication "$PUB" --early-numeric "$EARLY" --late-numeric "$LATE" \
  --side "$SIDE" --registers "$REGISTERS" --family "$FAMILY" --relisted "$C2" \
  --private "$PRIVATE" --results "$AGGREGATES"
```

The private outputs include the estimated risk indicators and a flag for each pupil placed only under the binding blind policy. The latter lets the complementary-record analyses describe this group. The published outputs are aggregate. Run `python3 summarize_response.py --results "$AGGREGATES" --institutional "$PUB/two-studies/institutional/results" --out "$AGGREGATES"` to verify all comparison rows using the published files alone.
