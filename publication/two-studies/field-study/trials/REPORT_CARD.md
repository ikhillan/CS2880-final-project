# When information changes the school a family eventually attends

Allende, Gallego and Neilson randomized 143 eligible preschools in three Chilean regions in 2010. The team visited 133 and delivered a bundled information package at parent meetings. It contained a video and a card naming up to 30 nearby primary schools. Schools above and below the national test score mean had different colors. The card highlighted five high-scoring schools. Two planned treatment intensities are pooled because receipt could not be distinguished reliably. Attendance at the meeting preceded exposure. Administrative data record the schools chosen in 2011. This study predates the centralized SAE used in the paper's four cohorts. [Design and main results](https://doi.org/10.3386/w35837) · [Report, map and coverage results, Sections O-3 and O-3.1](https://www.nber.org/data-appendix/w35837/SchoolChoiceInfoExp_R3_OnlineAppendix.pdf)

The outcome in Appendix Table O-4 columns 1–2 is the indicator that the school chosen was on the local report card. The study compares assignment to the card-and-video bundle with control. For responding parents who had yet to enroll, expanded-control estimates show a 15.9 percentage point reduction (preschool-clustered SE 3.3, 975 observations). The corresponding full-sample estimate is −11.2 points (SE 3.0, 1,775). The randomization-controls estimates are −17.2 and −11.8 points respectively. The main paper's Table I column 6 reports an increase of .099 in the normalized value added of the attended school in the pre-enrollment subsample (SE .037, 934 observations with data). Value added is a school characteristic. The difference between the endpoint samples, the clustered estimates, and the older decentralized setting matter when interpreting this comparison. The probability of attending any listed school and the quality of the school attended moved in opposite directions for this subsample.

The highlighted-five regression in Table O-4 columns 5–6 conditions on that pupil having selected a listed school. Its +.090 coefficient (SE .041, 639 pre-enrollment observations, expanded controls) pertains to a group chosen after assignment. It does not identify unconditional enrollment at a highlighted school. Comparable treatment and control base rates for joint top-five selection are not in the table. The supplement and the main paper report no counterfactual rank-blind and rank-informed offers and no randomized choice between those two menus.

## Reproduce the aggregate analysis

The [NBER paper supplement listing](https://www.nber.org/data-appendix/w35837/) contains a readme and the online appendix PDF. The reanalysis uses published regression coefficients and cluster-robust standard errors. It does not refit individual records. With Python 3 and Poppler installed, download the two PDFs at the cited URLs, then run

```sh
python3 check_report_card.py --paper report-card.pdf --appendix report-card-appendix.pdf --out report-card-estimates.csv
```

The extractor checks both original PDFs' SHA-256 digests, the tables, panel names and six-column structure. It independently extracts Table I, column 6 and Table O-4, columns 1, 2 and 6 for each of the three enrollment-timing groups. Table entries in the CSV use each study's original units. Confidence intervals use the normal approximation and the rounded published estimates. For the pre-enrollment group, an exploratory intersection–union sign check takes the maximum of the one-sided p-values for the two hypotheses of lower on-card attendance and higher value added. It gives p=.00373 under the normal approximation. This comparison was chosen after reading the authors' results and has no pre-specified or family-wide multiple-testing interpretation. Its outcomes differ from headcount placement.

The Chilean risk warnings analyzed by Arteaga et al. give a different intervention. The 2018–20 pop-up and supplementary WhatsApp warning ask families to add schools based on risk. The warning text does not supply a list of named schools. A Chilean higher-education trial by Larroucau et al. also has a different treatment. Its initial recommendation arm displayed four majors, as the Ministry did not authorize institution-specific recommendations in that arm. Its scaled intervention provided a searchable program directory and live cutoff estimates. Neither substitutes for a randomized comparison of the two K–12 menus.
