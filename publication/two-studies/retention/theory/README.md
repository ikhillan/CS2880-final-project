# Retaining baseline recipients while allowing school changes

The binary formulation in the paper is exact for strict pupil preferences and responsive program priorities on an explicit list of priority draws. Stability excludes outcomes that physically fit all pupils only because a program would have to reject someone who outranks one of its assignees. Applicant-optimality ensures that protecting every incumbent in *some* stable assignment suffices. Equal market-wide placement counts alone do not track which pupils kept a seat.

The reduction builds one common-order priority draw per graph edge plus a reference draw. All pupils receive a seat on the old menus on every draw. Each feasible additional historical full-list partner hit corresponds to selecting a distinct vertex. Switching both endpoints of an edge causes a pupil to lose their old seat. Thus maximizing a zero-exit menu realizes maximum independent set. A matching of m disjoint graph edges gives a gap of exactly m hits between preserving every old course and preserving every old recipient. The hardness is for an explicitly given set of lotteries. For the historical enrollment application a hit refers to a fixed observed enrollment school rather than a reference DA partner.

Run `python3 verify_retention.py` with standard-library Python and compare its JSON with `verified.json`. It enumerates all 9,000 three-pupil, two-program markets with ordered incomplete lists and strict priorities for the two capacity vectors (1,1) and (1,2). It checks 68,616 feasible assignments against the blocking inequalities and all eight incumbent masks in every market against the DA result. Independent direct enumeration of the reduction verifies 33,864 graph/menu pairs over all labeled graphs on 2 to 5 vertices. It checks the exact identities of possible exits, the safety of the reference draw, and coverage against actual full-list DA labels. No applicant records are used. All enumeration counts describe finite test families.

In a two-grade system with upper-grade assignments changing lower-grade sibling priorities, applying a stable witness relative to the old sibling placements can give the wrong test. To compose components before clearing, connect applicants to every program on either candidate menu, connect every applicant pair whose assignments can affect one another's priority or preference, and give each program its full typed capacity. A program's family status can also depend on the school-level identifiers of an older relative's assignment. Including the relative as a vertex (and all of their candidate-course edges) retains this dependence. Each clearing stage considers only its own resulting connected component. A proof follows by induction on grades and proposals, with fixed random priority keys shared between the component and whole-market runs. Capacities and the identities of the affected applicants are therefore local too. A global (pooled) capacity relaxation alone cannot establish seat retention.
