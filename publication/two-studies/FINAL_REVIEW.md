# Final internal review for ACM Economics and Computation

**I would argue for acceptance as a computational market-design paper.** A strong review can now rest on two auditable results. The first identifies when a platform can optimize menus independently for each family and when a small market-wide risk allowance couples those decisions. The second bounds how much of a placement deficit can be repaired even if every school accepts a voluntary reallocation. The remaining uncertainty in an EC review concerns the economic significance of a fixed-menu replay and the gap between this allocation model and the Chilean institution.

## What changed the assessment

- **Before the lottery.** On an explicit support, a zero-failure stability constraint separates by row. A fixed positive percentage of permitted exceptions makes the score decision NP-complete, even with one optional slot change. The reduction realizes the known set-union knapsack combinatorics with actual DA losses. On three national cohorts, protecting 1,024 simulated draws kept 29,018 of 67,909 proposed substitutions. No recorded-priority recipient lost a place. Eight comparison menus with identical edit counts caused 894–968 losses. The screen showed 27 fewer pupils their April school than the old menus. Even an oracle with their eventual enrollment labels could add at most 278 hits over the original menus under this protection, against a gain of 3,590 from the full switch. This bound makes the loss of predictive value interpretable. A fresh 768-draw evaluation found at least one lost place in 83 draws. The retrospective cohort and prospective randomness support different claims. [Proof](lottery-boundary.tex) · [Methods and evidence](design/risk/README.md)
- **After tentative offers.** The vacancy-chain construction improves incumbents weakly and adds 475 recipients in the four rank-informed school replays. Relaxing priorities and slot limits raises the ceiling to 649, far below the 1,787-place deficit. Restricting new options to the original top five cuts the ceiling to four. Both sides of the bound come from feasible flows and matching cut values. Repeating the same rule in NYC gave a different conclusion under modeled rental preferences. [Proof and table](chains.tex) · [Flow and independent checks](design/chains/README.md)
- **A genuine algorithmic baseline.** Joint cutoff coverage adds 9–30 seats over changing the frequency predictor’s tie-break alone across 24 independent-course comparisons. Under a common-lottery training kernel that difference shrinks to −4 to +2. The rank-blind predictor still places more pupils. This is a controlled mechanism comparison across three known cohorts. [Optimizers and results](design/portfolio/README.md)

## What I would defend in discussion

The informative number is **1,834**. These pupils remained eligible for their former course on their own new list and still lost their seat when everyone’s lists changed. The theorem traces each loss to a distinct other applicant with an edited menu under fixed capacities and priorities. It works for changes in both the graph and the application order, and separately for gains. Several sets of paths can certify the same allocations. Removing one witness's edit in isolation need not reverse its associated loss. [Proof](orders.tex) · [School and rental traces and verification](behavior/paths/README.md)

The prediction target is an actual enrolled school, observed in a register separate from the allocation replay. Most of the informed rule’s 4,638 net additional hits over rank-blind were among pupils who attended their initial offer school. Among 463,269 who enrolled somewhere else, the advantage was 586 and had the same sign in all four years. Against the first-five rule the model instead improved both aggregate endpoints. The comparison is informative precisely because the benchmark and the predicted label are explicit. [Annual comparisons](behavior/field/offer-concordance.csv)

The social-impact result is distributional. The 145,450 linked pupils with no initial offer were 7.39% of the linked cohort and accounted for 58.2% of the 1,609 net simulated losses. They also saw a small net decline in coverage of the school they actually attended. In the distinct group still without an offer after the wait list, most later attended outside their eligible original report. In NYC many low-income households have no affordable option in the sampled inventory. Both cases expose a limit on what re-ranking a fixed set can accomplish. No population effect is pooled across countries. [Historical linkage](behavior/field/README.md) · [Rental scenarios](behavior/nyc/README.md)

## What could still divide the committee

**Institutional fidelity.** The differences in replay placements are about one per thousand. Historical course-level disagreement with the pooled-capacity replay is 3.4–4.6%. Those percentages measure different quantities and cannot be subtracted to bound the policy effect. Even exact individual agreement need not extend to an applicant’s competitors. Of 66,719 applicants with different menus, only 11 were in a modeled competition component where the baseline assignment agreed for everyone. A discrepancy somewhere in a large component can be remote from a particular pupil. This check limits what historical fit can validate. [Independent component implementations](audit/components/README.md)

**Behavior.** Families never saw these proposed menus. Appending and reinserting the same option yield opposite first-choice findings. Under costless, fully informed truthful reporting with unrestricted applicant-proposing DA, restoring the original lists makes both policies coincide. A credible deployment claim would need the authority’s allocation implementation and observations of actual searches, submitted lists and enrollment under different advice, with a design that accounts for shared seats.

**Novelty.** Prior field work already shows that information changes search and that congestion can attenuate gains. Historical enrollment can reflect the actual allocation. The finite-scenario decision variable is the menu. The mechanism realizes a known overlap optimization problem. The new empirical contribution is measuring the loss of predictive value under protection and the upper limit of repair, with observed submitted ranks and independently linked enrollment. A proof, a finite check, and an empirical result play different evidentiary roles.
