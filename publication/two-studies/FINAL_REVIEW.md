# Final internal review for ACM Economics and Computation

**I would argue for acceptance as a computational market-design paper.** A strong review can now rest on two auditable results. The first identifies when a platform can optimize menus independently for each family and when a small market-wide risk allowance couples those decisions. The second bounds how much of a placement deficit can be repaired even if every school accepts a voluntary reallocation. The remaining uncertainty in an EC review concerns the economic significance of a fixed-menu replay and how families would respond to seeing different recommendations.

## What changed the assessment

- **Before the lottery.** On an explicit support, a zero-failure stability constraint separates by row. A fixed positive percentage of permitted exceptions makes the score decision NP-complete, even with one optional slot change. The reduction realizes the known set-union knapsack combinatorics with actual DA losses. In the original pooled-seat benchmark on three national cohorts, protecting 1,024 simulated draws kept 29,018 of 67,909 proposed substitutions. No recorded-priority recipient lost a place. Eight comparison menus with identical edit counts caused 894–968 losses. The screen showed 27 fewer pupils their April school than the old menus. Even an oracle with their eventual enrollment labels could add at most 278 hits over the original menus under this protection, against a gain of 3,590 from the full switch. This bound makes the loss of predictive value interpretable. A fresh 768-draw evaluation found at least one lost place in 83 draws. The retrospective cohort and prospective randomness support different claims. [Proof](lottery-boundary.tex) · [Methods and evidence](design/risk/README.md)
- **After tentative offers.** The vacancy-chain construction improves incumbents weakly and adds 475 recipients in the four pooled-seat school replays. Relaxing priorities and slot limits raises the ceiling to 649, far below the 1,787-place deficit. Restricting new options to the original top five cuts the ceiling to four. Both sides of the bound come from feasible flows and matching cut values. Repeating the same rule in NYC gave a different conclusion under modeled rental preferences. [Proof and table](chains.tex) · [Flow and independent checks](design/chains/README.md)
- **A genuine algorithmic baseline.** Joint cutoff coverage adds 9–30 seats over changing the frequency predictor’s tie-break alone across 24 pooled independent-course comparisons. Under a common-lottery training kernel that difference shrinks to −4 to +2. The rank-blind predictor still places more pupils. This is a controlled mechanism comparison across three known cohorts. [Optimizers and results](design/portfolio/README.md)

## What I would defend in discussion

The informative number is **1,834**. These pupils remained eligible for their former course on their own new list and still lost their seat when everyone’s lists changed. The theorem traces each loss to a distinct other applicant with an edited menu under fixed capacities and priorities. It works for changes in both the graph and the application order, and separately for gains. Several sets of paths can certify the same allocations. Removing one witness's edit in isolation need not reverse its associated loss. [Proof](orders.tex) · [School and rental traces and verification](behavior/paths/README.md)

The prediction target is an actual enrolled school, observed in a register separate from the allocation replay. Most of the informed rule’s 4,638 net additional hits over rank-blind were among pupils who attended their initial offer school. Among 463,269 who enrolled somewhere else, the advantage was 586 and had the same sign in all four years. Against the first-five rule the model instead improved both aggregate endpoints. The comparison is informative precisely because the benchmark and the predicted label are explicit. [Annual comparisons](behavior/field/offer-concordance.csv)

The social-impact result is distributional. The 145,450 linked pupils with no initial offer were 7.39% of the linked cohort and accounted for 76.0% of the 1,670 net simulated losses under the reconstructed Chilean rules. They also saw a small net decline in coverage of the school they actually attended. In the distinct group still without an offer after the wait list, most later attended outside their eligible original report. In NYC many low-income households have no affordable option in the sampled inventory. Both cases expose a limit on what re-ranking a fixed set can accomplish. No population effect is pooled across countries. [Historical linkage](behavior/field/README.md) · [Rental scenarios](behavior/nyc/README.md)

## What could still divide the committee

**Institutional fidelity.** Reconstructing the four original rounds from A1/B1/C1/F1 and checking the resulting predictions against D1 resolves the former course-level discrepancy. All 2,044,649 applicants match. Keeping the frozen shortlist comparisons, the institutional ceiling is 314 instead of 278. One menu profile per cohort attained the bound while preserving every detailed assignment in all 3,072 matched cohort–draws. The two family-lottery sensitivities give fixed-witness ceilings of 314 and 317. Exact fit on the original reports can coexist with uncertainty about the unobserved route through seat categories or how new recommendations would change behavior. The mechanism is credited to its designers and to the earlier published reconstruction. [Annual fits and counterfactuals](institutional/README.md)

**Behavior.** The 2022–25 reapplication records now identify the actual omitted courses some pupils named again. The rank-informed policy still loses 1,653 linked placements when these choices are appended. Most later search goes outside the original report. With newly named schools and common secondary vacancies, even priority-free headcount maximization in both arms leaves a gap of 1,558 pupils. The strongest qualification comes from selection into search. If the same 19.9% keep looking under both menus, it matters *which* pupils they are. The coverage comparison ranges from −179 to +5,412 when they append the first omitted school, and remains at least +920 when the added option is the least congested. The field warning motivates sensitivity to uptake but does not identify searches, new options or acceptance under these unoffered displays. [Study and reproducible outputs](behavior/response/README.md)

**Novelty.** Prior field work already shows that information changes search and that congestion can attenuate gains. Historical enrollment can reflect the actual allocation. The finite-scenario decision variable is the menu. The mechanism realizes a known overlap optimization problem. The new empirical contribution is measuring the loss of predictive value under protection and the upper limit of repair, with observed submitted ranks and independently linked enrollment. A proof, a finite check, and an empirical result play different evidentiary roles.
