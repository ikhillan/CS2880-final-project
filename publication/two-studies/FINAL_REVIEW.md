# Final internal review for ACM Economics and Computation

**My assessment is weak accept for an empirical market-design paper. I would not give it a strong accept on the current evidence.** The paper gives a verifiable, constructive account of displacement under two fixed policies and tests the same prediction on later national cohorts. A venue decision depends on the committee. This document is an internal assessment of the revision.

## What I would defend in discussion

The informative number is **1,834**. These pupils remained eligible for their former course on their own new list and still lost their seat when everyone’s lists changed. The theorem traces each loss to a distinct other applicant with an edited menu under fixed capacities and priorities. It works for changes in both the graph and the application order, and separately for gains. Several sets of paths can certify the same allocations. Removing one witness's edit in isolation need not reverse its associated loss. [Proof](orders.tex) · [School and rental traces and verification](behavior/paths/README.md)

The prediction target is an actual enrolled school, observed in a register separate from the allocation replay. Most of the informed rule’s 4,638 net additional hits over rank-blind were among pupils who attended their initial offer school. Among 463,269 who enrolled somewhere else, the advantage was 586 and had the same sign in all four years. Against the first-five rule the model instead improved both aggregate endpoints. The comparison is informative precisely because the benchmark and the predicted label are explicit. [Annual comparisons](behavior/field/offer-concordance.csv)

The social-impact result is distributional. The 145,450 linked pupils with no initial offer were 7.39% of the linked cohort and accounted for 58.2% of the 1,609 net simulated losses. They also saw a small net decline in coverage of the school they actually attended. In the distinct group still without an offer after the wait list, most later attended outside their eligible original report. In NYC many low-income households have no affordable option in the sampled inventory. Both cases expose a limit on what re-ranking a fixed set can accomplish. No population effect is pooled across countries. [Historical linkage](behavior/field/README.md) · [Rental scenarios](behavior/nyc/README.md)

## Why I would stop short of strong accept

**Institutional fidelity.** The differences in replay placements are about one per thousand. Historical course-level disagreement with the pooled-capacity replay is 3.4–4.6%. Those percentages measure different quantities and cannot be subtracted to bound the policy effect. Even exact individual agreement need not extend to an applicant’s competitors. Of 66,719 applicants with different menus, only 11 were in a modeled competition component where the baseline assignment agreed for everyone. A discrepancy somewhere in a large component can be remote from a particular pupil. This check limits what historical fit can validate. [Independent component implementations](audit/components/README.md)

**Behavior.** Families never saw these proposed menus. Appending and reinserting the same option yield opposite first-choice findings. Under costless, fully informed truthful reporting with unrestricted applicant-proposing DA, restoring the original lists makes both policies coincide. A credible deployment claim would need the authority’s allocation implementation and observations of actual searches, submitted lists and enrollment under different advice, with a design that accounts for shared seats.

**Novelty.** Prior field work already shows that information changes search and that congestion can attenuate gains. Historical enrollment can reflect the actual allocation. The defensible contribution is the combination of targeted, traceable displacement for fixed policies, an independently linked downstream label, and the observed limits of curation in the group without offers. A proof, a finite check, and an empirical result play different evidentiary roles. [School search](https://doi.org/10.3386/w32670) · [Equilibrium information effects](https://doi.org/10.3386/w35837) · [Field evidence on admission warnings](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjac013)
