# External validation and the school-family lottery

The benchmark is D1's initial course or no offer in 2022, 2023 and 2024. Use publicly documented rule choices and show the independent pooled comparison, the number of pupils with and without an official offer who change status, wrong courses among those placed by both, and subgroup fit. Keep D1 out of the counterfactual generator. The external paper reports zero historical errors. Replicate it if possible and report the absolute gap if any.

Keep the previous 1,024-draw design as the bridge. Add a separate school-level family lottery: draw a random permutation of observed F1 family components separately at each school and, within each component at that school, a uniform ordering of applicants. A pupil's ticket is shared across that school's grades and courses. F1 includes both common-guardian links and blood-sibling indicators. The public link component is the grouping assumption for the stress test. This is the design architecture described by Correa et al., sec. 4.1.2. Do not attribute the generated draws to Chile's historical administration. Compute the April-school ceiling at the same nested support sizes. Keep the original five-choice displays unchanged.

After reconstructing 2022–24, extend the historical check to 2025 using its published deterministic tie order. The lottery comparison retains the original three cohorts.

After checking the realized priority data for reversals of relative ranks across courses of the same school, add a course-specific family lottery with otherwise identical draws and group definition. The public-order reversals motivated this exploratory comparison.
