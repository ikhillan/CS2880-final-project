# Learning shortlist exposure from simulated full-market matches

Before inspecting policy outcomes we fixed the following experiment. A market has 64 renters and 64 listings. We use the existing calibrated generator, unit capacity, strict rankings, 40% budget cap, price coefficient 4 and individual noise 0.5. Lower-income renters with zero options remain in the ordinary sample. The enriched sample is conditional by construction and is reported separately. All donors are calibration donors. Only housing catalogs from June–August enter training or development. The January–March catalog follows the existing winter cleaning and removes identifiable summer overlaps. Both periods are familiar to the team. Independent simulation seeds do not create independent real-world households, periods or observations of housing assignments.

Each seed crosses 4 boroughs, 3 months and 2 designs. Record any insufficient-supply market individually. Inputs are observable attributes of feasible edges: asking rent relative to the budget, spare bedrooms (capped at two), distance, summer-referenced listing quality and the capped income-to-rent ratio. The last feature approximates the simulator's screening rule and could reproduce income screening in deployment. There are no renter, listing, or brokerage identifiers, private shocks, realized listing priorities, renter rankings, or group labels in the network. Training labels are each renter's partner in full-exposure renter-proposing deferred acceptance, with a separate outside class. A shared edge encoder is trained with a categorical cross-entropy over each renter's feasible listings and outside. A contextual version pools across renters and listings. The pointwise ablation uses no other renter's features. For each specification train three independently initialized models, select each checkpoint by validation cross-entropy and use the mean predicted probabilities; no test-driven choice of policy, epoch or seed.

Publish both top-five and coordinated versions. The latter anchors one edge per matched renter using a maximum-cardinality, then maximum-total-score feasible bipartite matching (outside dummies), then fills the remaining shortlist by the policy ranking. An anchor is exposure, not an allocation guarantee. Run the same decoder on the transparent observable score, a required simple allocation-aware comparison. Uniform uses the generator's seeded ordering. All eligible controls are paired on market, rankings, and uniform order. Separate the all-renter deployment from higher-income-only deployment where lower-income menus stay uniform. Primary outcomes: total matching rate under all-renter deployment and lower-income matching under higher-income-only deployment. Show both groups, the full-population denominator, and the top-five recovery rate of the simulated full-market DA partner amongst oracle-matched renters. Confidence intervals reflect simulation seed blocks conditional on the catalogs. They do not estimate sampling variation in NYC households, months or actual recommender effects.

The rollout calibration blocks are distinct from checkpoint validation. Freeze three initialization seeds (8101–8103) per architecture, width 32, 28 epochs, batch 32, AdamW with step size 0.002 and L2 decay 0.0001. The two contextual updates average feasible edge embeddings over each row and column and include both log degrees. The edge-logit head reads each updated pair; the outside head pools a renter's own edges. The pointwise ablation shares the encoder and both heads but drops the contextual updates. It therefore has fewer parameters. The experiment compares implementable information/architecture packages; it cannot isolate a pure information effect at equal capacity. Standardization moments come only from feasible training edges. Ensembled probabilities, averaged across the three individually selected checkpoints, are ranked. Stable index ties in the team's original transparent top-five order are retained. Other policy ties are resolved using independent market-keyed permutations.
