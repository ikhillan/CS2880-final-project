# A better prediction does not always produce more leases

Each number below refers to a simulated assignment. The ordinary sample draws from the borough-month catalog. The enriched sample deliberately stocks affordable units and draws lower-income renters who can afford at least one of them. All policies show at most five feasible listings per renter. Match rates include renters who cannot afford a single listing.

| Winter ordinary | 56.18 | 31.37 | 53.74 | 46.56 | 52.83 | 53.45 | 54.95 |
| Summer enriched | 66.39 | 36.10 | 61.60 | 59.13 | 65.21 | 68.99 | 69.93 |

The market network recovers the simulated full-market partner in 39.55% of oracle-matched cases in summer ordinary markets versus 17.58% under uniform exposure. It nonetheless matches 3.08 percentage points fewer renters overall (paired pointwise 95% interval −3.35 to −2.80). Both statements carry over to winter ordinary markets, with 38.27% versus 16.37% partner recovery and 3.35 points fewer placements (−3.68 to −3.02). In enriched markets the market model beats the pointwise network in placement by 6.08 points in summer (5.75 to 6.40) and 6.15 in winter (5.79 to 6.51). A simple observable maximum-cardinality assignment anchor is already strong in enriched markets. Its winter point estimate with the transparent score slightly exceeds the trained model with the same decoder.

A different intervention holds every lower-income renter's menu fixed at uniform and changes only the higher-income menus to the market network's top five. The lower-income match rate changes by −0.23 points (−0.38 to −0.08) in summer ordinary markets and −0.53 (−0.74 to −0.33) in winter ordinary markets. In enriched markets it increases by 7.51 points (6.94 to 8.09) and 7.47 (6.82 to 8.10). Even with these enriched gains, 4.95% of all lower-income renters lose a previously held placement in summer and 5.53% in winter. These are gross displacement rates across all lower-income renters and should not be interpreted as percentages of the smaller group who were initially matched.

The archived counts cover 3,072 calibration/test markets, eight policies and two rollouts, giving 49,152 policy-market-rollout records. The original simulator's serial DA independently reproduces match and displacement counts for all eight policies and both rollouts on the first and last full seed block of each partition (2,304 records). All archived score receipts link the model weights and input tensors to their SHA-256 fingerprints. All 3,072 full-market partner vectors in the calibration and test partitions were independently recovered by the batch DA implementation.

The independent summer CPU replay changed 434 of 73,728 top-five contextual menus and 130 renters' match status. Aggregate placement changed by 10 renters. For contextual menus with a coordinated assignment anchor, 2,445 menus and 650 match statuses changed, again with only 10 more renters matched in aggregate. Near-tied network and assignment scores matter when identifying who receives a particular listing. `predictions/` preserves the realized GPU scores used for the paper.

Giving the pointwise model two additional update layers and access to the renter's full feasible row narrows the placement gap. Both networks in this control have 11,906 parameters. Relative to the equal-parameter own-row model, the contextual model raises placements by 1.91 points (1.64 to 2.17) and 2.08 (1.82 to 2.35) in ordinary summer and winter markets, and by 2.82 (2.53 to 3.11) and 2.67 (2.35 to 2.99) in enriched markets. Full-partner menu recovery improves by 1.30, 1.42, 1.88 and 1.94 points respectively. This comparison was added after the main analysis, using the existing training and validation procedure and the same test markets; it is a follow-up ablation. The exact estimates and paired intervals are in `ablation-contrasts.csv`.
