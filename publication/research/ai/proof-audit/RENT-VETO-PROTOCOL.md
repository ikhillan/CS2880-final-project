# Optional veto on higher asking rents after a provisional offer

This follow-up responds to the completed contextual guardrail experiment: 860 of the 2,240 originally matched lower-income renter instances moving to a strictly better simulated rank would pay a higher asking rent. We fixed this evaluation after observing that finding and before running a rent veto. We evaluate the same 48 seed blocks, three months and four boroughs in each season and both designs, with unchanged five-slot displays to every applicant under the previously defined priority-blind guardrail.

We simulate universal take-up by initially matched lower-income applicants. Each consenting renter accepts proposals only to units in their original displayed set with asking rent no higher than their own original unit. Their own provisional unit remains acceptable. Other lower-income applicants keep their original proposal lists. Higher-income applicants retain the original guardrail's entire displayed and effective lists, including frozen original higher-income rows, with their full quota. Lower-income displayed units stay visible but consent can reduce the effective proposal count; report how many original recipients actually remove one or more edges and how many edges they remove. Compare the guarded allocation with and without this veto, tracing individuals rather than subtracting aggregate improvements.

Verify original provisional matches are stable on restricted original displays, every original recipient retains a weakly preferred matched unit, every consenting original recipient has nonincreasing asking rent, and all upper-group quotas and unchanged lower displayed lists are respected. Report total, higher- and lower-income placement rate changes for the veto versus guard with the same borough/month standardization and paired 5,000 seed-block bootstrap. Count original recipients who strictly improve versus their provisional unit in each run, who forgo an original guarded improvement entirely (return to original), who still improve at a different unit, and any newly improving recipients. Use exact source asking rents for the veto and audit, in dollars as represented by the archived FirstMover generator. Report new placements and effects on initially unmatched lower applicants. Do not infer actual demand for this policy from universal simulated take-up.
