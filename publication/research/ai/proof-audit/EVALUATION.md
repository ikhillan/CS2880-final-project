## Two-stage evaluation of protected placements

We use the final learned experiment's summer and winter test partitions and five-slot quota. Every market starts from a stable matching on everyone's uniform lists. Lower-income lists remain fixed; the alternative scores rank the higher group's eligible options. The score-aware repair consumes the original offers, the observable feasibility mask and the original displays. It does not use the private simulated rankings. A separate sensitivity arm also consumes exact simulated priority comparisons at held units. The latter answers what the platform could certify if priority information were available. Neither regime identifies real tenant outcomes.

The paired comparisons use the original learned pointwise and contextual top-five rules and the observable transparent-score control. We report previously matched lower-income recipients losing a placement, newly matched lower-income recipients, additional higher-income lists held at baseline by the closure, exactly how many higher-income rows switch, total placements and higher-income placements. We include every test market, including markets where scarcity leaves no lower-group offers to protect. Report separate designs and seasons; no choice of guardrail parameters or model is tuned on these data. The transparent comparison uses the original archived transparent ranking, including its stable index tie rule. Ties in learned candidate scores are broken by the market key used by the unguarded top-five implementation. We report raw counts for gross identity turnover and cluster paired differences by preexisting simulation seed blocks over the same borough and month, using identical paired multinomial bootstrap weights (5,000 resamples) to the main learned study. Each resample pools counts within month and borough, then combines cell-specific rates with borough household weights and equal month weights. Intervals capture simulation noise conditional on the catalogs.
