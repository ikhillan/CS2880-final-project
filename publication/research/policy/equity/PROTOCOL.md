# Distribution of placement losses in Chilean school choice

Written after the previously reported 2020/2021 cohort net placement and subgroup mean results, before computing the new individual transitions, selective rollouts, or reserved-seat sensitivity. This is a follow-up on both known test cohorts. No refit, cutoff selection or new data year. The original 2019-fitted rank-blind and rank-informed offer models and fixed random tie keys are used throughout. All comparisons below use five discretionary choices plus the protected returning-school fallback, as in the archived study.

## Migration between outcomes and opportunity among nonrecipients

Primary transition: change every pupil from the rank-blind to the rank-informed menus. Count paired baseline placed → new unplaced, baseline unplaced → new placed, and ordinal improvements/deteriorations (outside the reported list worst). Separately identify a nonrecipient under *full-list* DA: a new person entering as the result of truncation. For both policies count participants remaining placed among original full-list recipients, entrants from full-list nonrecipients, recorded-offer coverage, and full-list recipient loss. Keep distinct the denominators (prerollout recipients vs all applicants vs full-list nonrecipients).

Break results down by the ministry's B1 PRIORITARIO designation, which reflects an administrative vulnerability classification. It is not household income. Stratify by own list length ≤5 versus >5, and by a protected current-school option. Retain all applicants including zero discretionary choices. Report absolute counts, denominators and signed paired changes, with a separate count of people strictly worse or better conditional on both assignments being present. For comparisons between groups, calculate common-composition changes weighted by pooled group frequencies across exact cells of grade × length band (0–1, 2–5, 6–10, >10) × current-school entitlement, with cell coverage explicitly reported. Subgroup-by-grade summaries describe these fixed cohorts, not independent applicant sampling variation.

## Who creates spillovers?

Freeze an applicant's administrative group and use the same two rankers to construct four cohort-wide deployments: nobody switches, only priority-designated applicants switch, only other applicants switch, everyone switches. Both selective deployments are diagnostics, not proposals to offer one group inferior information. Report the within-group status transitions under each deployment. If only the opposite group switches, individual changes are pure spillovers. Decompose the uniform baseline-to-everyone change between the two editing groups by averaging the two switching orders (two-group Shapley accounting). An individual's own list may be unchanged even in the group whose rule switches. All subgroup-relative comparisons, including order-specific and averaged contributions, are evaluated within a single fixed national cohort and market interference is retained.

## Sensitivity to the allocation rule

The published pooled-seat model omits explicit routing of priority vacancies. Compare fixed scores and menus using a priority-sensitive school choice function. Each program retains returning applicants first; from open non-returning applicants it preferentially holds up to its published VACANTES_PRIORITARIOS number of applicants carrying PRIORITARIO=1; all remaining seats use the original coarse priority order/lottery and empty reserves release. The program's total capacity remains the original model's entitlement-protected vacancy count. The sensitivity is a simplified reserve-first rule, not the deployed legal algorithm. It abstracts from dynamic siblings and other reserves and places eligible priority applicants ahead of other non-returners in its priority seats. Compare both mechanisms' historical full-list fidelity in each group, matching outcomes, and selective-rollout effects. If the priority-sensitive specification fits the underlying administrative record worse, report that as well. Do not choose which specification to label primary based on the results.

Run exact deterministic full-market assignments and verify capacity, stability against the relevant program choice function, entitlements, archived pooled DA agreement and transition identities. Distribute only aggregates and replay programs. No participant identifiers, individual placements, enrollment/addresses or raw lotteries in deliverables.
