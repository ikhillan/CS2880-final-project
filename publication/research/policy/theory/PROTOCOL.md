# Capacity-constrained shortlists with a fixed prediction score

Specified after the previous paper established that the contextual top-five rankings lower visible matching capacity. Before observing the assignments of the new minimum-loss frontiers, apply the following to both original 1,152-market seasonal test panels. The contextual score is the exponential of the archived ensemble log probability, including the outside class in normalization. The decision uses the feasibility graph, probabilities and unfilled row quota only. It never reads the private preference order, group indicator or current realized allocation. Do not refit or select a frontier endpoint using outcomes. All applicants receive min(5, feasible degree) slots.

For each market, let v0 be matching capacity of its old contextual top five, and v1 capacity of its full feasible graph. For fractions 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and 1 use integer targets v0+ceil(f*(v1-v0)). Optimize total predicted full-partner probability on displays carrying an assignment of at least that size. For controls calculate original contextual, contextual with the paper's maximum-weight maximum-cardinality anchor, and uniform; use original archived masks for each. Primary quantities: ex ante predicted inclusion mass per renter, measured full-partner recovery among full-market DA recipients, all-renter actual simulated placements, lower-group placement and number reverting to original/full status. Report all fractions, with no resampling of training sets. Use the paper's cell weights and seed-block standardization. Predictive probabilities are outputs from a model trained in simulation; measured partner recovery can move counter to predicted mass. Reports/priority are used only in evaluating outcomes. In tied optimizers, use preexisting market-specific permutation to avoid systematically selecting small row indices. Conditional simulated cases and prior test panels are reused. No selection of best-performing arm for external validation.
