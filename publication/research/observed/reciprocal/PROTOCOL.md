# Columbia reciprocal-choice benchmark

Frozen before fitting or inspecting any policy outcome. The Harvard Dataverse study by Bhargava and Fisman supplies the universe of 16 sessions and the recorded yes/no responses. We checked that every keyed decision agrees with the accompanying extended questionnaire extract. Only the extended file supplies the overall liking score and pre-event profile. Use each side's recorded overall liking to rank mutually agreed pairs. Require a valid 1–10 rating in both directions, a reciprocal decision record and distinct participants on opposite sides. Keep every identifiable participant on the relevant proposing side, including those who have no such pair. Break tied scores by independently randomized pair-level priorities on each side. Multiple mutual yeses were allowed in the actual experiment. The one-partner capacity and DA outcomes are imposed by this study.

**Primary.** Use female proposers and a quota of five. Where the platform has no knowledge of affirmative decisions, select from all meetings with recorded decisions in both directions; intersect displayed pairs with the acceptable graph only when participants act on their recorded answers. The platform sees no same-event outcome or participant identifier in scoring features. For every market compare learned top-k against uniform recommendations, paired on the identical offered pool and true preference refinement. A separate two-stage check gives both policies the same mutually accepted and rated graph before choosing slates. This second comparison matches the housing benchmark's known-eligibility assumption but would require collecting decisions first. Report both irrespective of signs, along with an age-and-interests similarity baseline and the same learned ranking with one assignment anchor per matched proposer from maximum-cardinality/maximum-score matching on its available graph. No final assignment is reserved by an anchor.

**Prediction.** Train a regularized row-softmax model to predict the unit-capacity, full-visibility DA partner from pre-event attributes or the outside option. Training labels average over 32 seeded independent tie orders. Shared features are participant age, going-out and dating frequency, five self-descriptions, pairwise age difference, goal and field agreement, shared interests, five cross-person interest weights times self-descriptions and their reverse, and both sides' observed pool degrees. Standardize and impute using training only. Applicant and partner identifiers, dates, positions, yes/no decisions, mutual contacts, subjective partner ratings, and every follow-up measure are withheld from the primary predictor. Use a fixed L2 penalty 0.1 on coefficients in the mean negative-log-likelihood objective. Outside logit zero. No hyperparameter or cutoff chosen by test outcomes. Train a separate model for each proposer orientation and availability regime.

**Partition and uncertainty.** Train on the first eight eligible sessions (waves 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9) and test on waves 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 21. No same-day sessions cross the cutoff. Report equal test-session means of (i) retained full-partner rate among full-DA-matched proposers, (ii) recomputed placement rate among all proposers. Use 256 paired independent refinements and baseline list draws (same draw nesting across quota). Sensitivities use the other proposer side, k=1 and 3, and consent-known slates. Give individual test-session changes and paired 95% bootstrap-percentile uncertainty resampling event **days** with replacement, retaining all waves on each day and equal-weighting resulting waves, 20,000 draws with seed 2811. These few historical days cannot support strong population claims. The main sign pattern replicates only if partner recovery increases and placements fall. Evaluate the unknown-consent context directly against the observed real-world mutual-yes events by recording what share of all actual mutual yeses receive exposure; this outcome admits multiple partners. No multiple-testing inference on sensitivities.

Freeze precedes inspection of any fitting or benchmark outcomes. Input inspection checked schemas, keys, independent train/test date boundaries and decision concordance. No private participant-level outcomes will be included in the share archive.
